**Reflection:** The Agile Logbook was perhaps the most eye opening part of the project. Writing down our daily standup across multiple documents forced me to confront our bottlenecks honestly across all three weeks of development. My role included updating the logbook daily, setting up the Trello board and helping teammates overcome their struggles with the new software. Reflecting on this taught me that communication and transparency are the most critical parts of Agile. For example, when we hit a blocker on July 21 with team members not knowing Trello, we tracked it and the next day I adapted by creating detailed descriptions and labels for every card. Later, in Week 3, when supplier pricing delayed our CPE costing, the logbook shows how we pivoted to AP coverage mapping to keep momentum. By tracking our impediments, we were able to solve them as a team. This connects directly back to our Team Charter, where we promised to empower and respect each other's skill levels, proving that our Sprint Backlog wasn't just a static list but a living document we adapted to ensure we delivered a confident presentation and a solid technical architecture plan.
